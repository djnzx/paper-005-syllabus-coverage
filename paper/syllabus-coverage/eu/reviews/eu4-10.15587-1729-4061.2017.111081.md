# Review: Management of information protection based on the integrated implementation of decision support systems

**Authors:** Valeriy Lakhno; Valeriy Kozlovskyi; Yuliia Boiko; Andrii
Mishchenko; Ivan Opirskyy  
**Journal:** Eastern-European Journal of Enterprise Technologies, Vol. 5, No.
9 (89), 2017, pp. 36–42  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.15587/1729-4061.2017.111081

---

## 1. Problem

The authors target the operational management of information protection (IP) and
cybersecurity (CS) at objects of informatization (IO) —
information-communication systems and automated control systems facing a rising
number and complexity of targeted cyberattacks. Their core premise, drawn from
references [1–3], is that simply increasing the volume of protective means and
activities does not produce a proportional security effect and often only adds
to staff workload.

They identify a concrete gap in prior work. Expert-system risk models [9, 10] "
failed to introduce any application software to the market," and the decision
procedures of [11, 12] yielded "no hardware/software implementation." Commercial
DSS/ES tools [15, 16] are closed and costly for individual enterprises, while
non-profit alternatives "lack functionality."

The unifying problem, citing [17], is that the *integrated* implementation of
decision support systems (DSS) and expert systems (ES) "was not systematically
addressed in the context of management tasks for IS." The work is motivated by
the need to coordinate divergent expert opinions using interval estimates of the
degree of IO protection.

## 2. Research Tasks and Objectives

Section 3 states the aim: to develop a method and model for managing
cybersecurity at objects of informatization "based on the automation of a
procedure of coordination of expert opinions in a DSS." Two tasks follow: (1)
develop a method and model for managing IS through systemic DSS implementation;
and (2) design and test an IO cyber-security management DSS built on the *
*Delphi method** that accounts for IS interval estimates and metrics across
different classes of threats, anomalies, and cyber-attacks.

The scope is explicitly complementary, not replacing antivirus or
intrusion-detection systems — the DSS targets "complex situations … in which the
outcome of the task depends on subjective knowledge."

## 3. Methods

The proposed method comprises eight stages, from analyst task division (Stage 1)
and formalization of logical rules (Stage 2) through knowledge-base
compilation (Stage 3), data systematization (Stage 4), dynamic classifier
adjustment (Stage 5), compliance evaluation (Stage 6), generation of management
concepts (Stage 7), and long-term IPS planning (Stage 8), repeatable as needed.

Experts fill a questionnaire matrix $e_i$ of size $N \times MI$ (eq. 1),
where $N$ is the number of threat sources and $MI$ the number of techniques per
threat; round results aggregate into a summary matrix $e_r=\sum_{i=1}^{n} e_i$ (
eq. 2). Coordination uses the Delphi method extended with interval
estimates $\overline{ER}_{pse}=[\overline{ER}^-_{psew};\overline{ER}^+_{psew}]$ (
eqs. 3–4) correlated with IS metrics (eq. 5). Expert opinion significance is
weighted by
competence: $op_{pse}=\left(1-me(\overline{ER}_{pse},\tilde{ER})\right)\cdot C_{pse}$ (
eq. 6). Average and integral estimates (eqs. 7–10), a confidence-interval
radius $RA=\max(\widetilde{me})$ (eqs. 11–13), and a final summary
estimate $\widehat{ER}_{ps}=0.5\,(ES^+ + ES^-)$ (eq. 14) close the model.

The DSS, named **DMSSCSE**, is a web application built on MySQL, HTML, CSS, with
Python modules for graphical output. It was tested during IPS modernization at
enterprises in Kyiv, Lviv, and Chernihiv, engaging **7 to 11 experts** with ≥5
years' experience; a follow-up audit used analysts with ≥10 years' experience.

## 4. Key Results

Comparing expert assessments made independently versus through DMSSCSE, the
divergence of opinion among DSS-assisted experts was **about 15–18% lower** (
Figs. 3–4). Time spent on data processing with DMSSCSE was **35–50% less** than
independent analysis, while the number of rules engaged in the system's logical
output was **1.5× larger**, indicating richer inference.

The headline economic finding is a reduction in the cost of organizing
integrated IPS by **12–15%** versus alternative methods [2, 6, 10, 25],
reinforced in the abstract and conclusions. Time to assess threats fell by *
*11–12%**, and evaluation/response time to incidents by **11–14%**. The authors
also note that, without the DSS, experts rated protection too optimistically —
the audit aligned more closely with DMSSCSE estimates.

## 5. Conclusion

The authors conclude that the Delphi-based method and its DMSSCSE implementation
automate the generation of control-action variants and coordinate expert
judgments using interval estimates and IS metrics, improving organizational and
technical protection while cutting IPS costs by 12–15%. Future work targets
enriching the knowledge base and the logical-rule database as approbation
expands.

One caution: the discussion (Sec. 5) cites a 32–35% cost reduction for the
Kyiv/Chernihiv/Lviv computing centers, conflicting with the 12–15% figure stated
everywhere else — a reader should treat the savings as configuration-dependent.
The acknowledged limitation is dependence on independent experts familiar with
the specific IO during early knowledge-base formation.
