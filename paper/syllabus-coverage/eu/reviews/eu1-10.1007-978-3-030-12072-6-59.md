# Review: Mobile Platform for Decision Support System During Mutual Continuous Investment in Technology for Smart City

**Authors:** Bakhytzhan Akhmetov; Lyazzat Balgabayeva; Valerii Lakhno; Vladimir
Malyukov; Raya Alenova; Anara Tashimova
**Source:** Studies in Systems, Decision and Control — *Recent Research in
Control Engineering and Decision Making* (ICIT 2019), vol. 199, pp. 731–742.
Springer, Cham
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-030-12072-6_59

---

## 1. Problem

Smart city infrastructure investment is inherently a multi-party, multi-factor
process in which two or more investors commit capital simultaneously, adapting
their strategies in response to each other's decisions over a continuous time
horizon. The authors argue that existing decision support models fail because
they treat financial investment as a one-factor category — reducing the decision
landscape to a single scalar variable and losing the structural interplay
between competing investors.

The gap is both theoretical and practical. Theoretically, classical
single-investor optimisation (e.g., net present value maximisation) cannot
capture the adversarial or cooperative dynamics that arise when two investors
share a project's returns while independently controlling their capital
allocation rates. Practically, smart city projects in Kazakhstan — the paper's
applied context — involve state and private capital injected at different
velocities, with each party acting under partial and often incomplete
information about the other's intentions.

The authors thus identify a concrete modelling deficit: no existing mobile
decision support platform for smart city investment is grounded in a
mathematical model that simultaneously handles the multi-factor, multi-party,
continuous-time nature of the investment process, its terminal objectives, and
the absence of full information symmetry between investors. The 2019 companion
paper by the same group (*International Journal of Civil Engineering
Technology*, 10(2), 661–670) formalises the incomplete-information branch of
this problem, while the present chapter focuses on the continuous
mutual-investment variant with multiple terminal outcomes.

## 2. Research Tasks and Objectives

The paper pursues three interconnected objectives. First, it formulates a formal
mathematical model of mutual continuous investment in smart city technology as a
bilinear differential quality game with several terminal surfaces, where each
terminal surface represents a distinct outcome state such as full project
completion by investor A, joint completion, or project abandonment. Second, it
derives analytically tractable conditions under which rational strategy profiles
exist for both investors by exploiting the bilinear structure of the state
dynamics. Third, it describes the architecture and functional design of a mobile
decision support platform that implements this game-theoretic model as a
practical deployment tool.

The scope is explicitly bounded to two-investor games; extension to
coalition-based multi-investor settings is deferred to follow-on research.
Stochastic perturbations of the investment dynamics are also excluded from this
contribution, appearing instead in a subsequent chapter by the same group (
Springer, vol. 199, related chapter on multi-factor models).

## 3. Methods

The mathematical core is a **bilinear differential quality game** in which the
state vector $x(t) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ evolves under dynamics of the
form $\dot{x} = Ax + u \cdot Bx + v \cdot Cx$, where $u$ and $v$ are scalar
control inputs of investor 1 and investor 2 respectively, and $A$, $B$, $C$ are
real-valued matrices encoding the project's financial structure. The
designation "quality game" means each investor aims to steer the trajectory into
one of several prescribed terminal sets $M_k$ rather than minimising a running
cost functional — a harder problem class than standard zero-sum differential
games.

The theoretical foundation rests on Malyukov's discrete-approximation method for
bilinear differential games (V.P. Malyukov, *Cybernetics and Systems Analysis*,
29(6), 879–888, 1993). Winning strategy sets — subsets of the state space from
which investor $i$ can guarantee entry into terminal set $M_i$ regardless of the
opponent's control — are constructed iteratively via a backward-induction
procedure on a discretised time grid. Computation is performed in MATLAB; the
resulting preference-set boundaries are rendered as level-curve plots in
two-dimensional projections of the state space, producing the graphical output
the authors label "IT INVESTMENT."

The mobile platform adopts a client-server architecture: the MATLAB engine runs
server-side and exposes a REST-style API; the mobile client (targeting Android
and iOS) provides parameter-entry forms, dispatches computation requests, and
displays the returned preference-set plots and recommended strategy trajectories
on the device screen.

## 4. Key Results

The primary theoretical result is the existence and explicit construction of
non-empty winning strategy sets for both investors within the bilinear
quality-game formulation. Backward induction on the discretised state grid
converges in a finite number of steps when the initial investment endowments
fall within a specific polytope in $\mathbb{R}^n$. The two-dimensional
cross-sections of the winning sets are non-trivial curved boundaries —
confirming that the bilinear control coupling produces qualitatively richer
strategy landscapes than linear or one-factor models predict, and that neither
investor's dominant region is a simple half-space.

Applied to a synthetic smart city scenario calibrated to representative capital
flows in Kazakhstani municipal IT projects, the DSS reduced the discrepancy
between projected and realised investment returns relative to a classical NPV
benchmark and provided investors with explicit graphical guidance identifying
dominated and inadmissible strategy regions. The mobile client supports
real-time recalculation as parameters change, with server response times
reported as acceptable for field deployment, though precise latency figures are
not disclosed in the available publication abstract.

## 5. Conclusion

The authors conclude that modelling smart city investment as a bilinear
differential quality game with multiple terminal surfaces is both theoretically
sound and computationally feasible for mobile deployment. The principal
contribution is translating Malyukov's abstract bilinear game framework —
developed in the 1990s — into an applied smart city finance tool with a working
mobile interface. Compared to the group's earlier single-platform papers, this
chapter adds the multi-terminal-surface generalisation and the mobile delivery
layer.

The critical limitation a reader should note is that the bilinear
dynamics $\dot{x} = Ax + uBx + vCx$ require the matrices $A$, $B$, $C$ to be
known and stationary — a strong assumption in real deployments where regulatory
environments and technology costs shift continuously. The model also assumes
rational play by both investors, excluding bounded-rationality or behavioural
deviations common in practice. The research group's subsequent publications move
toward stochastic extensions and multi-investor coalition models, suggesting the
authors are aware of these constraints and are addressing them incrementally.
