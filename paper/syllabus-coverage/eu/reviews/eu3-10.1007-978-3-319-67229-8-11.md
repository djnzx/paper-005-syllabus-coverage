# Review: Development of a Support System for Managing the Cyber Security of Information and Communication Environment of Transport

**Authors:** Valeriy Lakhno; Alexander Petrov; Anton Petrov  
**Journal:** Advances in Intelligent Systems and Computing — Information Systems
Architecture and Technology: Proceedings of 38th International Conference ISAT
2017, pp. 113–127, 2018  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-319-67229-8_11

---

## 1. Problem

The authors target the operational management of cyber security in the
information and communication environment of transport — a class of critical
computer systems (CS) where an incident can disrupt traffic control, signalling,
and logistics. They argue that existing intrusion-detection and protection
approaches, including the intelligent monitoring systems and integrity models
cited from their own prior work and from El Hassani's Integrity-OrBAC, treat
protection-tool selection as a static design decision. Such schemes do not adapt
the response to the current operational state of the protected object.

The identified gap is the absence of decision support that chooses *rational
variants of reaction* to security events while accounting for live operational
data of the transport CS. Designers therefore over-provision protection,
inflating cost, or react too slowly to incidents.

The motivating question is concrete: how can a support system generate
protection-method sets that are simultaneously compatible with a given critical
system, cost-efficient, and fast enough to inform a responsible operator about
an incident in time?

## 2. Research Tasks and Objectives

The stated objectives are: (1) to build a model for operational management of an
information object's cyber security; (2) to formulate a morphological method
that generates rational sets of protection methods matched to a specific CS; (3)
to define an objective function balancing security against cost; and (4) to
implement and validate the model as decision-support software (referred to as
the IDSS / DMSS system).

The scope is deliberately limited to the *selection and configuration* of
protection methods and to incident-notification timing. The work does not claim
a new detection algorithm; it composes existing controls into optimal modular
sets for the transport domain.

## 3. Methods

The core methodology is a **morphological approach**. For each security
perimeter of the information object, a morphological matrix enumerates candidate
protection methods; combinations across matrices yield alternative
protection-set variants. A hardware–software compatibility check filters
infeasible combinations so that selected tools actually interoperate.

Variant choice is driven by an objective function that **maximises the ratio of
a consolidated "information security" index to consolidated "costs"**. The
security level $LS$ is estimated as a noisy-OR style product over
information-object crosspoints:

$$LS = 1 - \prod_{i} \left(1 - A_s^{i} \cdot A_t^{i} \cdot TL_i \cdot ICR_C\right)$$

where $A_s^{i}$ and $A_t^{i}$ capture data-criticality and breach levels, $TL_i$
the trust level, and $ICR_C$ a result coefficient per crosspoint. The framework
is embedded in an intelligent decision-support system that ranks feasible sets
and surfaces incident information to a responsible individual.

## 4. Key Results

The reported evaluation of the implemented software (DMSS for critical
information systems) shows a **32–35% reduction in the cost** of building the
information-security system relative to alternative configuration approaches.
Residual attack risk for protected nodes is driven down to the **1.78–1.91%**
range, and incident-response/notification time is shortened by roughly *
*6.9–7.2×**.

The optimisation locates an efficient expenditure band of about **$5,200–$5,500
** for critical nodes, illustrating the cost–security trade-off the objective
function is designed to balance. These outcomes map directly onto objectives (3)
and (4): the morphological generator plus the ratio-maximising function deliver
cheaper, faster-responding protection sets.

The contribution rests on validation at transport companies (Dnipro) and
industrial enterprises (Kyiv), demonstrating practical applicability rather than
a purely theoretical construct.

## 5. Conclusion

The authors conclude that a morphological, decision-support–driven method can
generate protection-method sets that are compatible with transport critical
systems while cutting both build cost and operator notification time. They
position future work toward broader sectoral expert/decision-support systems in
cyber security, extending the morphological catalogue to additional domains.

A reader should treat the headline percentages with caution: the gains depend on
the chosen baseline and on expert-assigned coefficients ($TL$, $ICR_C$,
criticality levels) in the $LS$ formula. The morphological combinatorics can
also explode for large objects, so the practical reach of the optimisation under
realistic perimeter counts remains an open question.
