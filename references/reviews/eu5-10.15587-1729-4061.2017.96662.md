## 1. Problem

The authors target operational control over the protection of an object of
informatization (OBI) — a complex organizational-technical structure operating
under uncertainty about the state of its information-communication system (ICS).
The motivating phenomenon is the growing volume and complexity of cyberthreats,
which makes static protection planning inadequate.

They identify a precise gap in prior work. Mathematical DSS/ES models in [1–4] "
are not brought to employable software products," while the intelligent IPCS
of [9, 10] are proprietary, so customers remain unaware of the methods forming
controlling influences [11]. Papers [12, 13] list concrete shortcomings: the
need for highly qualified experts, difficulty adapting models to a given
organization, inability to evaluate a deployed IPCS, and dependence on reliable
incident statistics.

The central problem, drawing on [16, 17], is that existing IS-management
standards "do not form specific approaches to managing the cyberprotection of
OBI." The work asks how to plan a rational structure of an
information-protection system (IPS) and select responses to security events
under incomplete knowledge of attacker impacts.

## 2. Research Tasks and Objectives

Section 3 sets the aim: a model for counteracting cyberattacks via an
intelligent decision-making support system (IDMSS) that selects rational
response variants "with regard to operational data on the state of OBI." Three
tasks follow: (1) design an IPCS architecture with both centralized and
decentralized processing; (2) improve an operational-control (OC) model that
raises IS-management efficiency under uncertainty and improves IPS-structure
planning; and (3) build and test an IDMSS software complex.

Scope is explicitly preventive and complementary — the IDMSS plans
protection-means composition rather than replacing antivirus or
intrusion-detection layers.

## 3. Methods

The IPCS is built on a threat model expressed as a tuple of business processes,
information arrays, resources, vulnerabilities, users, information flows, and
OBI states (eq. 1), with the level of security $LS$ as the controlled variable.
Protection is organized across **five perimeters** — information system (PIS),
control of OBI (PCOI), user access (UAP), network equipment (PNE), and outer
perimeter (OPIO).

A **morphological approach** drives rational-set selection: morphological
matrices enumerate protection-means per functional subsystem, and a pairwise
compatibility function $s(CM_{lm},CM_{pr})\in\{0,1\}$ truncates the Cartesian
product (eqs. 14–15) to software–hardware-compatible combinations. The objective
function maximizes the ratio of the "protection of information" indicator to
the "expenditures" indicator (eq. 16), with weights from the **T. Saaty AHP
method** over matrices of pairwise comparisons. Structural-technological
resource optimization minimizes failure probability subject to duplication,
distribution, time $T^*$, and memory constraints (eqs. 2–3).

Quantitative protection
uses $LS_{CIS}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-C_{ICR}\cdot At_i\cdot As_i\cdot TL_i\cdot LS_i)$ (
eq. 4), and overall protection $LS=1-\overline{R}=1-\sum_s (C_s/C_\Sigma)P_s$ (
eq. 20), with unauthorized-access-channel probabilities for internal (3
perimeters) and external (5 perimeters) intruders (eqs. 21–25). Fuzzy
inference (Matlab Fuzzy Toolbox) resolves weakly-formalized situations, and the
rational response is $RV^{rat}(P_{CA})=\arg\min OF(RV_i,z)$ (eqs. 8–11).

## 4. Key Results

The software complex **DMSSCIS** was tested during IPS modernization at
enterprises in Chernihiv (2016), Dnipro (2014), Poltava (2013–2014), and
industrial enterprises in Kyiv. After forming a rational IPM composition, the
predicted risk fell to **1.78–1.91%**, on average **5.9–6.2× lower** than the
risk of previously used IPS.

The cost–risk curve (Fig. 4) shows a pronounced minimum: at an IPS cost of about
**5200–5500 conditional units** for critical nodes, the probability of an
intruder reaching all aims is $10^{-2}$; spending above ~13 000 units yields no
meaningful gain. Time to inform decision-makers about IS incidents dropped *
*6.9–7.2×**. Table 1 quantifies this per attack class — e.g., for remote
perimeter attacks the mean decision time fell from **27–35 min to 2–3 min**, and
for DOS/DDOS from 15–20 min to 5–7 min.

Overall, the approach reduced IPM expenditures by **32–35%** versus
alternatives [2, 6, 10, 25].

## 5. Conclusion

The authors conclude they delivered an IPCS architecture whose perimeter-wise
protection sets are chosen by an objective function maximizing
protection-to-cost, a morphological operational-control model accounting for
software–hardware compatibility, and a verified DMSSCIS complex cutting planned
IPS spending by up to 35%. Future work targets tighter integration between
traditional cybersecurity mechanisms and DMSSCIS modules.

One caution: the model leans on expert-built membership functions and production
rules at the initial stage (the acknowledged shortcoming), so the headline risk
and cost figures are conditional on expert quality and on the conditional-unit
cost scale, which the paper never maps to currency.
