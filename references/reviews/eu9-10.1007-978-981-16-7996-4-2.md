# Review: Modeling and Optimization of Discrete Evolutionary Systems of Information Security Management in a Random Environment

**Authors:** V. A. Lakhno; D. Y. Kasatkin; O. V. Skliarenko; Y. O. Kolodinska  
**Journal:** Smart Innovation, Systems and Technologies (Machine Learning and
Autonomous Systems), Vol. 269, pp. 9–22, 2022  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-16-7996-4_2

---

## 1. Problem

The authors treat an information security management system (ISMS) as a
*discrete evolutionary system operating in a random environment* — a setting in
which the state transitions of the protected object (incidents, recoveries,
reconfigurations) are governed by stochastic, memory-bearing dynamics rather
than by deterministic schedules. The keywords make the framing explicit: "
information security management system," "queuing system," and "Markov and
semi-Markov chains."

The gap they identify is methodological. Much prior stochastic cybersecurity
modeling — e.g., Hoffmann's Markov models of cyber kill chains, Ficco's Markov
behavioral models for IoT malware, El Bouchti & Nahhal's stochastic game nets
for SCADA, and Xu & Hua's Markov-based cyber-insurance pricing (all in the
chapter's 25 references) — relies on *memoryless* Markov assumptions or on
colored Petri-net reliability blocks. These cannot faithfully capture sojourn
times whose distributions are non-exponential, which is exactly what semi-Markov
dynamics demand.

The central problem the chapter sets is therefore the **stability and optimal
control of the zero solutions of a system of nonlinear difference equations
whose coefficients depend on a semi-Markov chain** — i.e., when does an ISMS
modeled this way return to (or stay near) its secure equilibrium, and how should
control be chosen to guarantee that.

## 2. Research Tasks and Objectives

The stated objectives, condensed in the abstract, are threefold: (1) to propose
a method for studying the stability of the zero solutions of nonlinear
difference equations that depend on a semi-Markov chain; (2) to reduce that
stochastic stability question to the analysis of *deterministic moment
equations* derived for the system; and (3) to obtain the **necessary optimality
conditions** for such systems, turning the stability analysis into a basis for
optimizing security-management decisions.

The scope is mathematical and methodological. The chapter promises a
stability/optimality apparatus for the random-environment ISMS abstraction; it
does not claim a deployed software tool or a large empirical validation,
positioning itself instead as the analytical foundation that the authors'
applied decision-support work (cf. their EEJET papers on DSS for cyber
protection) can rest upon.

## 3. Methods

The mathematical core is a system of **nonlinear difference equations**
modulated by a **semi-Markov chain** — the discrete-time evolutionary system.
Because direct analysis of such stochastic recursions is intractable, the
authors apply the **method of moment equations**: they derive deterministic
equations governing the statistical moments (expectations and higher-order
moments) of the state vector, and study the stability of the zero solution
through those deterministic surrogates. Stability of the moment equations then
certifies stability (in the corresponding moment sense) of the original
random-environment system.

On top of the stability analysis, the authors build an **optimization layer**,
deriving necessary optimality conditions — the chapter is framed around "
modeling *and optimization*." This couples the semi-Markov ISMS model to a
control objective, consistent with the "queuing system" keyword: the ISMS is
treated as a queue-like service process in which security events arrive and are
processed under randomly switching regimes.

The surrounding citation base situates the technique among stochastic
dependability methods — stochastic activity networks for smart-grid control
centers (Zeng et al.), automated colored-Petri-net reliability block diagrams (
Robidoux et al.), and predictive exploitability frameworks (Abraham & Nair) —
against which the semi-Markov moment-equation approach is offered as a more
general treatment of non-exponential sojourn times.

## 4. Key Results

The chapter's principal result is the **proposed stability-analysis method
itself**: a procedure that establishes the stability of the zero solutions of
the semi-Markov-dependent nonlinear difference system by analyzing its
deterministic moment equations. This is the theoretical deliverable that
addresses objectives (1) and (2).

The second reported result is the derivation of **necessary optimality
conditions** for these discrete evolutionary systems (objective 3). Together
these convert a qualitative "is the ISMS stable?" question into a constructive
criterion that can, in principle, guide the choice of management actions so the
protected system remains in its secure equilibrium under random disturbances.

Because the published abstract and metadata carry no numeric benchmarks,
accuracy figures, or simulation sizes, the contribution is best read as
analytical rather than empirical: the value claimed is the generality of the
semi-Markov/moment-equation formulation over the memoryless Markov and Petri-net
models that dominate the cited literature, not a measured performance gain.

## 5. Conclusion

The authors conclude that stability of an information-security-management system
operating in a random environment can be rigorously assessed via the
deterministic moment equations of an underlying semi-Markov-driven nonlinear
difference system, and that necessary optimality conditions follow, giving a
route to optimizing security management under uncertainty. The natural
continuation — consistent with the group's applied decision-support research —
is to embed these conditions in computational ISMS/queuing tools and to validate
them on real incident-arrival data.

One caution for the reader: the publicly available record is the abstract,
keywords, and reference list only (the Springer full text is paywalled), so the
specific form of the moment equations, the optimality conditions, and any worked
example cannot be verified here; moment-stability also does not by itself
guarantee almost-sure (path-wise) stability, an assumption practitioners should
check before relying on the result.
